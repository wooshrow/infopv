PRISM Tutorial

  1. Download and install PRISM on your computer, if you have not done this yet in Week-1.

  2. Do the first tutorial, Knuth Die, from this tutorial. Then, do one more DTMC tutorial, namely Herman, and the Dining Philosophers tutorial for MDP. Track your findings (more on this is specified below).

  3. Present your findings from doing these tutorials in a Report of about 4-6 pages. A neat report is expected. Use Latex, here is a nice template. If you use Overleaf you can immediately copy the template. Try to write the report narratively rather than just presenting a bullet list of findings.

    • For Knuth Die: your findings on the last three sections: Model checking with PRISM, Statistical model checking with PRISM, and Expected termination time.
      Give a one paragraph summary what the algorithm is about, along with a citation to the paper where more information on the algorithm can be found.
      Include the graphs that you produce there and give a discussion on the graphs.
      For the last section, also include the math that leads to your hand calculated results.

    • For Herman: your findings on the number of states of herman7.pm and your findings on the last two sections: Reaching a stable state and Initial configurations (where you experimentally verify a certain unproven conjecture).
      Give a one paragraph summary what the algorithm is about, along with a citation to the paper where more information on the algorithm can be found. Include the graph/s that you produce there and give a discussion on the graph/s.

    • For Dining Philosophers:
      Give a one paragraph summary what the Dining Philosophers problem is about.
      Give a summary how Lehmann-Rabin randomised solution works.
      Present your results of analysis of Lehman-Rabin and that of its Courteuos variant.

    Few formatting tips to improve the neatness of your report:

    1. Use fully aligned paragraphs (no-jagged right).
    2. Avoid using overly large margins. This decreases the information density.
    3. Figures and tables are centerly aligned. Figures-width should be smaller than your text-width.
    4. Pay attention to the size of a figure and its information content. Showing a large picture with little content makes an impression that you try to bloat the report/paper.
    5. If you number a figure like "Figure 2.1: probabilities over different epsilon", then you should refer to it from your main text (else there is no need to number it).

  4. Hand in your documentation by simply emailing a PDF to me.
