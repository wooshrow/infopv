PRISM Tutorial

  1. Download and install PRISM on your computer, if you have not done this yet in Week-1.

  2. Do the first tutorial, Knuth Die, from this tutorial. Then, do one more: either the DTMC tutorial Herman, or the Dining Philosophers tutorial for MDP. The parts to focus on are specified in more details below.

  3. Present your findings from doing these tutorials in a Report of about 3-4 pages. A neat report is expected. Use Latex, here is a nice template. If you use Overleaf you can immediately copy the template. Try to write the report narratively rather than just presenting a bullet list of findings. Apply academic writing. So, surpress colloquial styles, e.g. write full out "does not" instead of "doesn't". An AI-written report will be rejected.

    • For Knuth Die: focus on your findings on the last three sections: Model checking with PRISM, Statistical model checking with PRISM, and Expected termination time. More specifically:
      (1) Give a one paragraph summary what the algorithm is about, along with a citation to the original paper that presents the algorithm. The paper itself is sadly nowhere to be found online. But give the citation anyway, at least to give the credit. The citation entry can be found in the tutorial.
      (2) Include the graphs (two) that you produce in the sections Model checking with Prism and Statistical model checking with Prism. Give a discussion on each graph.
      (3) For the last section, include the math that leads to your hand calculated results. How does PRISM's output match the number you get from your math calculation?

    • For Herman: focus on your findings on the last two sections: Reaching a stable state and Initial configurations (where you experimentally verify a certain unproven conjecture). More specifically:
      (1) Give a one paragraph summary what the algorithm is about, along with a citation to the paper where more information on the algorithm can be found.
      (2) Your finding for the Section Reaching a stable state.
      (3) Your finding for the Section Initial configurations (about the unproven conjecture). Give the produced graph and some discussion on it.

    • For Dining Philosophers:
      (1) Give a one paragraph summary what the Dining Philosophers problem is about.
      (2) Give a summary how Lehmann-Rabin randomised solution works, along with a citation to the original paper that presents the algorithm
      (3) Present your results of analysis of Lehman-Rabin and that of its Courteuos variant.

    Few formatting tips to improve the neatness of your report:

    1. Use fully aligned paragraphs (no-jagged right).
    2. Avoid using overly large margins. This decreases the information density.
    3. Figures and tables are centerly aligned. Figures-width should be smaller than your text-width.
    4. Pay attention to the size of a figure and its information content. Showing a large picture with little content makes an impression that you try to bloat the report/paper.
    5. If you number a figure like "Figure 2.1: probabilities over different epsilon", then you should refer to it from your main text (else there is no need to number it).

  4. Hand in your documentation by simply emailing a PDF to me.
